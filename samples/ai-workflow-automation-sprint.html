Question: Where does our team lose the most hours to manual work, and what can we automate safely?
Sample report. Marlowe Precision Parts is a fictional company, and every account, figure and finding below is illustrative. It shows the structure, depth and labelling of a real deliverable, not results for a real client. In client work, every observation links to its public source.

1. Bottom line

~14 hrsa week spent re-typing quote requests and building the weekly sales report
2processes mapped end to end with the people who run them
1pilot designed, using tools the company already pays for
  • Quote requests are the biggest time sink. Each one arrives by email, is re-typed into a spreadsheet and then again into the ERP.
  • Most of the work is copying, not judgment. Pricing decisions take minutes; moving data between systems takes the rest.
  • A safe pilot is possible without new core systems. The ERP already accepts imports, and the email platform supports automation.

So what: Automate the capture and entry of quote requests first, with an estimator approving every quote before it is sent.

2. Workflow map: quote requests

Observed From interviews with the sales coordinator and two estimators. Times are their estimates for a typical week.

Email arrives→Coordinator re-types into spreadsheet→Estimator prices it→Coordinator re-types into ERP→Quote PDF emailed

4. Pilot design: opportunities 1 and 2

Email arrives→Details extracted to a form→Missing info requested automatically→Coordinator checks the form→Estimator prices and approves→Quote sent
  • Tools: the existing email platform, a low-code automation tool and a shared spreadsheet the team already uses. No change to the ERP in the pilot.
  • Human approval: nothing reaches a customer without the coordinator's check and the estimator's sign-off.
  • Success measure: time from request to quote, and re-typing hours, tracked for four weeks against the current baseline.
  • Exit: if accuracy is below what the coordinator can trust, the pilot stops and the manual process continues unchanged.

6. Confidence and caveats

  • Hours are the team's own estimates, not timed measurements. The pilot's first task is to confirm the baseline.
  • Build cost and risk are relative ratings. A pilot build is quoted separately after this sprint.
  • We left price suggestions out on purpose: pricing is the estimators' expertise, and errors there are costly.
