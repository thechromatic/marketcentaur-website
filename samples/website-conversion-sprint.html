Question: Why does steady website traffic produce so few booking requests, and what should we fix first?
Sample report. Brightwater Home Services is a fictional company, and every account, figure and finding below is illustrative. It shows the structure, depth and labelling of a real deliverable, not results for a real client. In client work, every observation links to its public source.

1. Bottom line

3 of 5conversion pillars scored 2 or below out of 5
9 → 4booking-form fields, after removing questions the team never uses
1 of 6buyer questions where an AI assistant named the business
  • Visitors arrive with the right intent. Service-area pages match what local customers search for, and most traffic lands on the right page.
  • The site then makes booking harder than it needs to be. Three competing calls to action, a phone number that can't be tapped on mobile, and a nine-field form slow people down at the moment they are ready to act.
  • Trust is earned elsewhere but not shown here. Strong reviews and a trade accreditation exist, but visitors have to leave the site or scroll to the footer to see them.

So what: The fastest gains are in fixes 1–4 below: one clear call to action, a tappable phone number, a shorter form and visible reviews. None needs a redesign.

2. Five-pillar scorecard

Observed Each pillar is scored from 1 (blocking inquiries) to 5 (working well), with the evidence behind the score.

PillarScoreEvidence
Value proposition3 / 5The headline "Quality you can trust" doesn't say what the business does or where. Services appear only below the first screen.
Call-to-action clarity2 / 5Three different buttons above the fold ("Get a quote", "Contact us", "Learn more"). The phone number is an image, so it can't be tapped.
Trust signals2 / 5Over 200 reviews on the business profile, none shown on the site. The accreditation number sits in the footer only.
UX friction2 / 5A nine-field booking form with a required "How did you hear about us?" field, and no line saying when the team will reply.
Visitor fit4 / 5Service-area pages match local search terms. Emergency visitors reach the emergency page in one click.

3. Top-10 fix list

Recommendation Ranked by expected impact on inquiries, then by effort. Impact is our judgment from the evidence above, not a forecast.

#FixImpactEffort
1Use one primary call to action site-wide: "Book a visit"HighLow
2Make the phone number a tappable link in the mobile headerHighLow
3Cut the booking form to four fields: name, phone, postcode, jobHighLow

Interpretation Assistants that named competitors cited their review pages and service-area pages with prices and response times. Fixes 8 and 9 address the same gap on this site.

6. Mobile and form review

  • Observed On a mid-range phone, the booking button sits below the first screen on every service page.
  • Observed The form's error messages appear only at the top of the page, so mistakes in lower fields go unnoticed.
  • Observed The postcode field opens a full keyboard instead of the numeric one where postcodes are numeric.
  • Recommendation Add a sticky "Book a visit" bar on mobile and show errors next to the field that caused them.

7. Confidence and caveats

  • Scores reflect a review of the live site and public profiles, not analytics. With analytics access, a real sprint also checks where visitors drop off.
  • AI answers change between sessions and over time. The check is a snapshot that shows patterns, not a ranking.
  • Impact ratings are our judgment. We don't promise a specific increase in inquiries; results depend on traffic, pricing and competition.
