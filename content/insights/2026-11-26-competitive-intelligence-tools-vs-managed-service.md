Once a business decides to track competitors properly, the next question is how. There are three broad options: do it yourself with free tools, buy competitive intelligence software, or use a managed service where someone else does the monitoring and analysis.

Each one works for a different kind of company. Here's how to choose.

## Option 1: Do it yourself

**What it looks like:** Google Alerts, free page-change monitors, newsletter subscriptions, a spreadsheet, and a fixed 30 minutes a week to review.

**Works well when:**

- You have three to five competitors that matter
- Someone on the team has a little time each week and the discipline to keep it up
- Budget is tight and you want to learn what's worth tracking before spending money

**Watch out for:** it depends on one person's habits. When they get busy, it stops. And the collection is easy to set up, but the "so what" step, which is the valuable part, is the first thing to get skipped.

## Option 2: Competitive intelligence software

**What it looks like:** a platform that monitors competitors' websites, news, reviews and social channels, then shows changes in a dashboard and sends alerts. Many include battlecard tools for sales teams.

**Works well when:**

- You track many competitors across several markets
- You have someone, often in product marketing, who owns competitive intelligence and has time to work in the tool
- Your sales team needs battlecards kept up to date

**Watch out for:** software collects and organises. It doesn't decide what matters for your business. Without an owner, alerts pile up unread. Pricing for dedicated platforms is often aimed at mid-sized and larger companies, so check whether the cost fits the number of decisions it will actually inform.

## Option 3: A managed service

**What it looks like:** an outside team monitors your competitors and sends a regular brief, usually weekly or monthly, covering what changed, why it matters and what you might do about it.

**Works well when:**

- No one internally has time to own competitive intelligence
- You want analysis and recommendations, not another dashboard
- You want a consistent rhythm without hiring an analyst

**Watch out for:** an outside team won't know your business as well as you do at the start. Look for a provider that asks about your upcoming decisions, cites sources for every claim, and adjusts the brief based on your feedback.

## Four questions to help you decide

**1. Who will own it?** If you can name a person with a few hours a week, do-it-yourself or software can work. If you can't, a managed service is more likely to keep running.

**2. How many competitors and markets?** A handful in one market is manageable by hand. Dozens across several regions needs software or outside help.

**3. What do you need at the end: data or decisions?** Software gives you data. A managed service gives you a brief with recommendations. Do-it-yourself gives you whatever you have time to produce.

**4. Which decisions will it inform?** List the pricing, product, market and sales decisions coming up in the next six months. Choose the option that will actually feed into those, not the one with the most features.

## A sensible path for most growing businesses

1. **Start with do-it-yourself** for a month to learn which signals matter in your market.
2. **If it keeps getting dropped,** hand it to a managed service or assign a clear owner.
3. **Add software** once you're tracking enough competitors that manual collection becomes the bottleneck.

Whatever you choose, judge it by one test: did it change a decision this quarter? If not, change the approach.
