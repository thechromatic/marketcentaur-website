India's Digital Personal Data Protection Rules arrived in stages, and the stage most businesses care about is now six months away. If your business collects names, phone numbers, emails, addresses or payment details from people in India, this applies to you, whatever your size.

This is a practical plan, not legal advice. Your own counsel should confirm what applies to your business.

## What applies when?

The Rules were notified by the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology on 13 November 2025 and apply in three phases ([AZB & Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/update-indias-digital-personal-data-protection-framework-comes-into-effect/)):

| Date | What applies |
|---|---|
| 13 November 2025 | The Data Protection Board, definitions and procedures |
| 13 November 2026 | Registration and duties for consent managers, and the Board's powers over them |
| **13 May 2027** | The main framework: notice and consent, duties of businesses that handle data, children's data, data principal rights and cross-border rules |

For most businesses, the November 2026 phase changes little directly. It is the May 2027 phase that needs preparation.

## What do the main duties ask of a business?

In plain terms, a business that decides why and how personal data is used (a "data fiduciary") will need to:

- **Give a clear notice.** Separate from other information, listing the personal data collected, the purpose, and how people can withdraw consent, use their rights and complain to the Board.
- **Get specific, informed consent** where consent is the basis, with the notice given before or with the request.
- **Protect the data.** Reasonable safeguards such as encryption or masking, access controls, logging and monitoring, and backups, with security duties written into contracts with vendors who process data for you.
- **Report breaches.** Tell the Board without delay, follow with a detailed report within 72 hours (or longer if the Board allows), and tell affected people without delay. CERT-In and sector regulators have their own reporting rules on top.
- **Handle requests.** Let people access, correct and erase their data and raise grievances.
- **Take extra care with children's data**, including verifiable parental consent.

Larger platforms and businesses designated as significant data fiduciaries have further duties, such as annual impact assessments and audits.

## What does a six-month plan look like?

Spread the work so nothing lands in the final month.

1. **November–December: map the data.** List every place personal data comes in (website forms, WhatsApp, billing, CRM, HR, events), where it is stored, who can see it and which vendors touch it. A spreadsheet is enough.
2. **January: fix notices and consent.** Rewrite privacy notices and consent wording for each collection point, in plain language and in the languages your customers use.
3. **February: review vendors.** List processors such as your CRM, email tool, cloud storage, payroll and support desk. Check their contracts for security and breach-notification terms.
4. **March: set up breach handling.** Write a one-page breach process: who decides, who tells the Board and customers, and how the 72-hour report is prepared. Run a short practice drill.
5. **April: set up requests and retention.** Decide how people ask for access, correction or erasure, who answers, and how long each kind of data is kept.
6. **May: train and check.** Brief staff who handle personal data, and check every item above before 13 May.

## What can be automated?

Quite a lot of the routine work. Forms can capture consent with a timestamp and the notice version shown. Requests can be logged and routed to the right person with a due date. A data map can be kept current from a short monthly check. The judgement calls, such as whether a purpose is lawful, stay with people and your counsel.

## FAQ

### Does the DPDP Act apply to small businesses?

Yes. It applies to digital personal data handled in India, and to some processing outside India linked to offering goods or services to people in India. There is no general exemption by business size, although some duties apply only to significant data fiduciaries.

### What started on 13 November 2026?

The rules for consent managers: registration, their obligations and the Board's powers over them. Most businesses are not consent managers, so the main preparation is for 13 May 2027.

### How quickly must we report a personal data breach?

The Rules require notice to the Data Protection Board without delay, followed by a detailed report within 72 hours unless the Board allows longer, and notice to affected people without delay. Other regulators, including CERT-In, have their own timelines.

### Do we need new software to comply?

Not necessarily. Most small businesses can meet the main duties with clear notices, updated forms, a vendor list, a breach process and a simple request log, using tools they already have.
