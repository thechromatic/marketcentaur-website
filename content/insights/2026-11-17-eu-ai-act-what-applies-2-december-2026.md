Many businesses read "EU AI Act delayed" in the summer and moved it down the list. Part of that is right: the high-risk rules did move. Part of it is not, and one date falls in a few weeks.

This guide sets out what the amending regulation changed, what still applies, and a short checklist for any business that builds, sells or uses AI that reaches people in the EU. It is a practical summary, not legal advice.

## What did the AI omnibus change?

The amending regulation, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, was published in the EU's Official Journal on 24 July 2026 and entered into force on 27 July 2026 ([White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/eu-ai-omnibus-enters-force-amending-ai-act)). Its main effects:

| Topic | What changed |
|---|---|
| Stand-alone high-risk systems (Annex III: for example biometrics, employment, education) | Obligations now apply from **2 December 2027** |
| High-risk systems that are safety components of regulated products (Annex I: for example medical devices, machinery) | Obligations now apply from **2 August 2028** |
| AI literacy (Article 4) | Softened: providers and deployers must now "take measures to support" a sufficient level of AI literacy, rather than ensure it |
| Marking of generative AI output | Systems placed on the market before 2 August 2026 have until **2 December 2026** |

The deferral covers the high-risk chapter only. Most of the transparency rules in Article 50 still apply from 2 August 2026.

## What applies on 2 December 2026?

The grace period ends for generative AI systems that were already on the EU market before 2 August 2026. From 2 December, their outputs (text, images, audio or video) must be marked in a machine-readable way so they can be detected as AI-generated. Systems placed on the market from 2 August 2026 onwards had to comply from that date.

This mainly affects providers: companies that build or sell a generative AI product or feature. If you only use someone else's tool, your supplier carries this duty, but it is worth asking them how they meet it.

## Which transparency duties apply now?

Article 50 sets out a small number of duties that matter to ordinary businesses, not only AI developers. In plain terms:

- **Chatbots and assistants.** People should be told when they are interacting with an AI system, unless it is obvious.
- **Generated content.** Providers of generative AI must make outputs detectable as AI-generated (the marking duty above).
- **Deepfakes.** Deployers who publish realistic AI-generated or manipulated images, audio or video of people, places or events must disclose that it is artificial, with limited exceptions such as clearly artistic work.

If your website chat, voice assistant or marketing content reaches people in the EU, these are the duties to check first.

## Does it apply to businesses outside the EU?

It can. The Act applies to providers placing AI systems on the EU market and, in some cases, to providers and deployers outside the EU whose AI output is used in the EU. An Indian, UK or US company selling an AI feature to EU customers, or running a chatbot for EU users, should not assume it is out of scope. Your own counsel can confirm how it applies to you.

## What should a business do this month?

You do not need a large programme to cover the near-term duties. Four steps are enough to start:

1. **List where you use AI.** A one-page register of tools, what each is used for, what data goes in and who reviews the output. Our free [toolkit](/toolkit) includes an AI-use register template.
2. **Check every assistant that talks to the public.** Make sure your website chat, WhatsApp or voice assistant says clearly that it is automated, and offers a route to a person.
3. **Ask your AI suppliers about marking.** If you sell a product that uses a third-party model, ask how its outputs are marked and how that carries through to your product.
4. **Flag anything that could be high-risk.** Uses in recruitment, education, credit, or access to essential services need closer review before December 2027. Note them now.

Then support AI literacy in a proportionate way: short, practical training on your real tasks, and clear rules on what staff should never paste into an AI tool.

## FAQ

### Has the EU AI Act been delayed?

Partly. The high-risk obligations moved to 2 December 2027 and 2 August 2028. Prohibited practices, transparency duties and the rules for general-purpose AI models were not deferred by the omnibus.

### What is the 2 December 2026 deadline?

It is the end of the grace period for marking the outputs of generative AI systems placed on the EU market before 2 August 2026. From that date, their outputs must be marked so they can be detected as AI-generated.

### Do we still need AI literacy training?

The duty is now to take measures to support a sufficient level of AI literacy among staff who use AI on your behalf. Short, task-based training and clear usage rules are a sensible way to show that.

### We only use ChatGPT or Copilot internally. Does any of this apply?

The marking duty falls on the tool's provider. Your main tasks are knowing where AI is used, keeping a person reviewing important output, and disclosing AI where it talks to customers or creates realistic content about people.
