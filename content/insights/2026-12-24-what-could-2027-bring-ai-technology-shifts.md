Every autumn the large research firms publish their predictions for the coming year. They are useful, but they are not facts. Each one is an analyst's view of where things are heading, and some will turn out wrong.

We read the 2027 forecasts from Gartner, Forrester and IDC so you don't have to. This post sets out where they agree, what that means for a growing business, and which 2027 dates are already fixed in law. Where we give our own view, we say so.

## Which shifts matter most for a mid-sized business?

Most of these forecasts are written for very large companies. Here are the five we think carry over to a business with 10 to 500 people. This is our opinion, based on the sources above.

1. **Agents move from demos to daily work.** Every firm expects AI agents to handle more routine, multi-step work: chasing invoices, triaging enquiries, updating records. The question for 2027 is less "should we try agents?" and more "which workflow first, and who checks the output?"
2. **AI costs need an owner.** Usage-based pricing means an agent that runs more often can cost more without any price change, which is IDC's point. Someone should own a monthly view of AI tool spend against the work it replaces.
3. **Trust becomes a buying factor.** Forrester expects buyers to lose confidence in providers whose claims they cannot verify. For a smaller business, published methods, clear samples and honest limits matter more than they did.
4. **Your business is described by AI before a buyer visits you.** If AI assistants are shortlisting suppliers, what they say about you shapes the shortlist. Checking that is now a routine marketing task.
5. **Governance moves from policy to practice.** The setbacks Forrester predicts come from governance gaps, not weak models. A short AI use policy, a register of tools and a named reviewer for each workflow prevent most of them.

## Which 2027 dates are already fixed?

Forecasts can be wrong. Legal deadlines are known. If your business operates in or sells to these markets, put these in the calendar now:

- **13 May 2027:** the main duties under India's DPDP Rules (notice, consent, security, breach reporting and data principal rights) begin. See our [six-month DPDP plan](/insights/dpdp-what-to-finish-before-may-2027).
- **2 December 2027:** the EU AI Act's obligations for stand-alone high-risk AI systems, such as AI used in hiring or education, apply after the 2026 amendment moved them. See [what applies and what moved](/insights/eu-ai-act-what-applies-2-december-2026).

Neither is legal advice. Your own counsel confirms how each applies to you.

## What about energy use and sustainability?

AI uses real electricity. The International Energy Agency's *Energy and AI* report projects that global data centre electricity demand will more than double by 2030, to around 945 TWh ([APPA summary of the IEA report](https://www.publicpower.org/periodical/article/electricity-demand-data-centers-worldwide-set-more-double-2030-iea)). For most businesses, AI's share of their own footprint is small. Still, customers and larger clients increasingly ask about it in supplier questionnaires. A short note on which AI tools you use, and why, is easier to give now than to reconstruct later.

## How should you read technology forecasts?

Our view: treat a forecast as a prompt to ask a question, not as a plan. A prediction that "75% of B2B purchases" will be influenced by agents doesn't mean three in four of your buyers. It means it is worth asking a few customers how they research suppliers now.

Watch where independent firms agree. In 2027 the common ground is clear. Agents will do more work, costs and governance will matter more, and buyers will rely more on AI. The details, and the percentages, are where forecasts most often miss.

## FAQ

### Are these predictions facts?

No. They are forecasts published by Gartner, Forrester and IDC in late 2026. They reflect each firm's analysis and may not happen as described. The regulatory dates in this post are the only fixed points.

### Do these forecasts apply to small and mid-sized businesses?

Most are written about large enterprises, but the direction carries over. Agents taking on routine work, closer attention to AI costs and buyers researching through AI all affect smaller firms, usually a little later.

### What is the single most useful thing to do before 2027?

List the AI tools your business already uses and who owns each one. It is the base for governance, cost control and any agent pilot, and for most teams it is a short exercise.

### Will AI agents replace staff in 2027?

The forecasts point to roles changing rather than disappearing overnight. IDC, for example, expects a share of junior and mid-level roles at large firms to shift towards supervising AI agents by 2029. In our experience, the near-term change is in tasks, with people reviewing more and repeating less.
