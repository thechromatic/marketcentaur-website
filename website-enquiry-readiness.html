Is your website making it easy to inquire?

Answer six practical questions to spot possible friction between a visitor landing on your website and taking the next step. You’ll get a directional score and a short list of areas to review.

About 2 minutesBased on your answersNo website scan or data upload

Quick readiness check

For each statement, choose the answer that best reflects your website today. If you are unsure, choose “Partly / not sure” and treat it as a prompt to investigate.

1. Is your offer clear within a few seconds?

Can a new visitor quickly understand who you help, what you do and why it matters?

2. Is your intended audience specific enough?

Do your main pages speak to the needs of the customer groups you most want to reach?

3. Is the next step obvious and relevant?

Can visitors find an appropriate action such as requesting a sample, booking a conversation or trying a tool?

4. Does the website give visitors reasons to trust the offer?

Are services, process and limitations explained accurately, with relevant evidence where available?

5. Is the experience usable on a mobile screen?

Are navigation, text, buttons and forms easy to use on a phone?

6. Is it easy to complete an inquiry and understand what happens next?

Does the form ask only for useful information and explain how the inquiry will be handled?

This self-check is a directional guide, not an audit. It does not crawl your website, test form delivery, assess analytics or predict inquiry volume. Validate the suggested priorities against real visitor behavior and business goals.

What to do with your summary

  • Fix the "Not yet" answers first. They are usually the cheapest, fastest improvements, such as a clearer headline or a shorter form.
  • Check the "Partly / not sure" answers with real behavior. Watch where visitors drop off in your analytics, or ask two or three recent customers how they found the next step.
  • Test on a phone. Try to complete an inquiry yourself on mobile, start to finish, and note every point where you hesitate.
