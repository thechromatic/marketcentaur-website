Your Business Now Has a Profile in ChatGPT. Is It Right?

OpenAI now lets businesses claim a ChatGPT profile, add product feeds and launch an agent. What changed, what to check first and how to measure it.

By The MarketCentaur Team13 November 20265 min read
Short answer

On 10 November 2026 OpenAI launched Businesses in ChatGPT: eligible businesses can claim and verify a profile, add product feeds with prices and availability, see how often they are mentioned and, in the US, launch a business agent. Before claiming yours, check what ChatGPT already says about you, fix the facts it relies on and record a baseline.

For two years, the question for most businesses was whether AI assistants mention them at all. That question now has a second half. On 10 November 2026 OpenAI launched a beta called Businesses in ChatGPT, which lets companies claim a profile inside ChatGPT, supply product data and, in some cases, run their own agent there (PYMNTS).

That makes an AI assistant look less like a search engine and more like a directory and a shop counter. Here is what changed, and a short list of what to check before you touch anything.

What exactly did OpenAI launch?

According to the launch coverage, the beta has three parts:

  • Business Hub. Companies can claim and verify a profile, update their business information and supply product feeds with pricing and availability. Shopify merchants can connect their store directly.
  • Engagement insights. Aggregated data on how often a business is mentioned in ChatGPT answers, clicks to its website and common conversation topics. OpenAI says businesses will not see individual conversations, and data appears only above set thresholds.
  • Business Agents. After claiming a profile, an eligible business can build and test an agent that answers questions about its products and services. People who see the business in an answer can open its profile and talk to that agent.

At launch, Business Agents are described as available to eligible US ecommerce and online-service businesses, with more markets and categories planned (Dataconomy). If you sell outside the US, that is a reason to prepare now rather than a reason to ignore it.

Why does the timing matter this month?

Two things are happening at once.

Google began rolling out its November 2026 core update on 10 November and says it may take up to two weeks, which puts the end close to 24 November (Search Engine Journal). Black Friday falls on 27 November. Search rankings may move during the busiest week of the year for many stores.

At the same time, Adobe forecasts that traffic to US retail sites from AI assistants will rise about 130% year on year this holiday season (Forbes). More shoppers are asking an assistant first and visiting a site second.

Our view: this is a good month to know exactly what AI assistants say about you, because both channels may shift at once.

What should you check before claiming a profile?

Claiming a profile is quick. Getting the facts behind it right takes longer, and assistants draw on those facts whether or not you claim anything. Work through these five checks first.

  1. Ask the questions your buyers ask. Write ten to twenty real questions, such as "best [product] for [use] under [budget]" or "who installs [service] near [city]". Run them in ChatGPT and other assistants. Record whether you are named, how you are described and who is recommended instead.
  2. Check the facts that are quoted. Note every fact an assistant states about you: prices, opening hours, locations, product names, delivery areas, return policy. Mark anything wrong or out of date.
  3. Trace the sources. Where an assistant cites sources, list them. Wrong facts usually come from an old page, a directory listing, a marketplace listing or a review site, not from nowhere.
  4. Tidy your product data. If you sell products, check that names, identifiers, prices, stock and key attributes match across your site, your feed and any marketplaces. A product feed only helps if it agrees with everything else.
  5. Decide what an agent may say. If you plan to use a business agent, write down which questions it should answer, which it must hand to a person, and where its approved answers come from.

Keep the results in one spreadsheet. That is your baseline.

How do you measure whether it worked?

Rankings and clicks will not tell the whole story, because many AI answers never send a click. Track three things against the same question set, once a month:

Measure What to record
Presence How many of your questions name you
Accuracy How many quoted facts about you are correct
Sources Which pages and listings assistants cite for you and for competitors

If you claim a profile, add OpenAI's engagement insights alongside, once your activity passes its thresholds. Wait until Google's core update has finished before judging changes in search traffic; Google itself advises waiting until a rollout completes before reviewing Search Console data.

FAQ

Is Businesses in ChatGPT available outside the United States?

At launch, the business agents feature was described as available to eligible US ecommerce and online-service businesses, with other markets planned. Check OpenAI's own pages for current eligibility in your country.

Do we need to claim a ChatGPT profile to appear in answers?

No. ChatGPT already describes many businesses from public sources. A claimed profile and product feed give you more control over the facts, but the public pages and listings it reads still matter.

Will a product feed guarantee that ChatGPT recommends us?

No. Answers change and depend on many factors. Accurate, consistent product data improves the facts assistants can use; it does not guarantee a recommendation.

Should we wait for Google's core update to finish before acting?

Wait before judging traffic changes, but not before checking your facts. Fixing wrong prices, hours or product details helps in search and in AI answers either way.
