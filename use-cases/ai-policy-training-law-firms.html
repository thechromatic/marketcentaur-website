An AI-use policy and practical training for law firms

For law firms whose people already use AI tools, often without shared rules.

Short answer

Staff are using AI tools, and clients are starting to ask how. We run hands-on workshops built around your firm's real tasks, and help you set up a plain-English AI-use policy and register that record which tools are allowed, for what, with which data. Your own counsel reviews the policy; we do not give legal advice.

The problem

What usually goes wrong

  • Use of AI tools is uneven, and nobody is sure what is allowed.
  • Clients ask about AI use and confidentiality, and answers vary.
  • Training is generic and does not reflect the firm's actual work.
How it works

How we approach it

01 / STEP

Discovery session

We meet a few team members to collect real tasks and current tool use.

02 / STEP

Hands-on workshop

Practical sessions on research, drafting support and checking output, built on your tasks.

03 / STEP

A plain-English AI-use policy and register, drafted for your firm to review.

04 / STEP

Follow-up

A short follow-up to answer questions and refine the prompt library.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Do you give legal or regulatory advice?

No. We provide practical training and draft documents for your firm to review. Your own counsel decides what applies.

Can the workshop run on-site?

Yes. Workshops run on-site or online, as a half day, a full day or a short programme.

What should staff never put into an AI tool?

The policy sets this out for your firm, typically covering client-confidential information and any data your approved tools are not cleared to handle.

Should we bill clients for time saved by AI?

That is for your firm and its regulators to decide. Guidance is emerging: in November 2026 the New York State Bar adopted a report that treats general AI tools as overhead, billable only where matter-specific and disclosed (ABA Journal). Our AI Use & Billing Policy Pack drafts options for your counsel to review.
