Illustrative opportunity briefFictional example
Observed developmentA target company announces a new regional office.
Why it may matter

The move could indicate geographic expansion. It does not, by itself, establish a need for your product or service.

What to verifyMarket fit and current priorities

Check whether the new region matches your ideal customer profile, confirm relevant business context, and identify the roles responsible for the initiative.

Recommended next stepDecide whether the account merits attention.

Compare the account against agreed qualification criteria. If the fit is strong, prepare a relevant, human-reviewed approach; otherwise, keep it off the priority list.

This is a fictional example created to demonstrate report structure. It is not a real company finding, a verified buying signal or a client result.
What a brief includes

Evidence, context and next steps

A useful report separates what was observed from what it might mean. Key findings are accompanied by source notes where available, relevance to the agreed audience, and a suggested next research step.

Designed around your market

Scoped to the decision you need to make

The company profile, geography, volume and level of detail are agreed before work begins. Reports are tailored to the market and business question rather than produced as generic lists.
