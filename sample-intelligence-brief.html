AI search and the shrinking click

Question: Is AI search changing how buyers find businesses like ours, and what should we do about it?

1. Bottom line

8% vs 15%Google users clicked a regular result on 8% of visits when an AI summary appeared, versus 15% when none appeared1
51%of B2B software buyers now start their research in an AI chatbot4
+693%year-over-year growth in traffic from AI tools to U.S. retail sites during the 2025 holiday season6
  • Buyers increasingly get answers without clicking. When Google shows an AI summary, clicks to websites fall sharply, and several independent studies agree on the direction.
  • The shortlist is forming inside AI assistants. Business buyers now use chatbots to discover and compare vendors before they ever visit a website.
  • The traffic that does arrive is better qualified. AI-referred visitors are growing fast from a small base and tend to be more engaged, so your website has fewer, more valuable chances to convert.

So what: Being cited in AI answers, and converting the smaller number of visitors who do click, now matters more than raw ranking position.

2. What we observed

Observed Seven data points from five independent sources:

SignalEvidenceSource
Fewer clicks from AI summariesIn browsing data from 900 U.S. adults (March 2025), users clicked a traditional result on 8% of visits with an AI Overview, versus 15% without one. Only 1% of visits included a click on a link inside the summary. About 18% of searches showed an AI summary.Pew Research Center1
Top-ranked pages lose the mostOn keywords that trigger an AI Overview, click-through for the #1 organic result was 58% lower in December 2025 than it would have been otherwise, up from a 34.5% gap measured in April 2025.Ahrefs, 300,000 keywords2
Paid clicks fall tooAcross 3,100+ queries for 42 organizations, organic CTR on AI Overview queries fell 61% and paid CTR fell 68% (Sept 2024 to Sept 2025). Organic CTR also fell 41% on queries without AI Overviews.Seer Interactive3
Being cited paysBrands cited inside an AI Overview earned about 35% more organic clicks and 91% more paid clicks than uncited brands on the same results page.Seer Interactive, as reported3b
B2B research starts in chatIn a March 2026 survey of 1,076 software buyers, 51% start research in an AI chatbot and 71% rely on chatbots for software research (up from 60%). 69% ended up choosing a different vendor than they first planned because of chatbot guidance.G24
Beyond softwareA quarter of B2B buyers now use generative AI more than search for vendor research, and nearly two-thirds use it at least as much. Adoption is much higher among U.S. buyers than in other regions.Responsive, 350+ buyers5
Consumer shopping shifts tooTraffic from generative AI tools to U.S. retail sites rose 693% year over year in Nov–Dec 2025. Shoppers arriving from AI assistants were 33% less likely to bounce.Adobe Analytics6

For scale, OpenAI reported about 900 million weekly ChatGPT users in February 2026, more than double the figure a year earlier.7

3. What it likely means

Interpretation The research step of the buying journey is moving from the results page into the AI answer. Three consequences follow for a typical small or mid-sized business:

  • Visibility is now about being mentioned, not only being ranked. If an assistant names three suppliers and you're not one of them, a strong Google position helps less than it used to. G2 also reports that buyers think more highly of vendors that chatbots recommend.4
  • Fewer visits, higher stakes per visit. Visitors who click through from an AI answer have often already compared options. A vague homepage, slow mobile page or hard-to-find contact form now loses a better-qualified buyer.
  • Buying more ads doesn't fully solve it. Seer's data shows paid click-through falling on the same queries, so shifting budget from SEO to ads doesn't escape the trend.3

4. Who is most exposed

Interpretation Based on the query types most likely to trigger AI summaries (longer, question-style searches1) and the buyer survey data:

Business typeExposureWhy
B2B software, IT and tech servicesHighBuyers in this sector use AI for vendor research the most5, and comparison questions are the top chatbot use case4.
Professional services (consulting, agencies, accounting)HighTraffic depends on "how to" and "best firm for" content, the question-style queries most likely to show AI summaries.
Ecommerce and D2CMediumInformational traffic is at risk, but AI referrals are growing fast and arrive with more intent6.
Local services (trades, clinics, salons)Lower for nowShort "near me" searches trigger AI summaries less often. Accurate listings and reviews still drive results.

5. Recommended actions

Recommendation In order of effort, lowest first:

  1. Audit your AI visibility (week 1). Ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity the 10 questions your buyers actually ask, such as "best [service] for [type of company] in [region]". Record whether you're named, how you're described and which competitors appear.
  2. Fix the pages AI tools quote (weeks 1–4). Make sure your homepage and service pages state plainly who you serve, what you do, where, and for whom. Add clear FAQs, pricing guidance or ranges, and comparison content. Assistants summarize clear, specific, factual pages more easily.
  3. Strengthen third-party proof (ongoing). AI answers draw on review sites, directories, forums and press, not only your own site. Keep listings consistent and ask happy customers for detailed reviews.
  4. Raise conversion on the traffic you keep (weeks 2–6). With fewer clicks, each visit matters more. Tighten the headline, cut form fields, add trust signals and make the next step obvious on mobile.
  5. Measure the new funnel (month 2+). Track AI referrals separately in analytics, monitor branded search volume, and re-run the visibility audit every month.

6. Confidence and caveats

  • Direction: high confidence. Pew, Ahrefs and Seer used different methods and all found fewer clicks when AI summaries appear.
  • Size of the effect: medium confidence. Estimates range widely depending on query type, position and method. Google has disputed Pew's methodology.1b
  • Forecasts are not facts. Gartner predicted in 2024 that search engine volume would drop 25% by 2026 as people turned to AI chatbots.8 Other analysts questioned that forecast at the time.9 We treat it as a directional signal, not a measured result.
  • Sample limits. The G2 data covers software buyers only, and Pew and Adobe cover U.S. users. Behavior in other markets may lag or differ.
